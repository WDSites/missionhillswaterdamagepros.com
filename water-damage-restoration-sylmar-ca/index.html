When water is spreading in Sylmar

Spreading water has a few signs. It darkens the carpet in a second room, it lifts a laminate floor edge, it drips from a ceiling fixture or it keeps rising after you have shut a valve. Any of those means the loss is still growing, and the time to call is now, whatever the hour.

Before we arrive in Sylmar

Close the main valve

Turn the main shut-off clockwise until it stops. If it is stuck, call anyway and say so.

Switch off power with care

Cut the breaker for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground. Otherwise stay out and call the utility or 911.

Raise what you can

Put furniture legs on blocks or foil, lift electronics and keep papers off the floor.

Keep clear of dirty water

If water came from a sewer line or from outside, children and pets should stay away from it.

Moving water on a slope

Sylmar rises from just over 1,100 feet near San Fernando Road to just over 1,700 feet at Hubbard Street, by published accounts of the neighborhood. That is about 600 feet of climb across a community that backs up to the Tujunga Canyons. Water behaves differently on a slope, because it enters from above and moves toward the lowest door, garage or slab.

That makes yard-side clues important. If water entered under a back door or through a garage, note where it came from and take photos before cleaning. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows flood zones by address, and it is the right place to check yours instead of guessing from the street. Water that comes from outside is handled on the flood damage restoration page.

Pipes, earthquakes and older homes

Sylmar knows what a shaking ground can do to utilities. After the 1971 San Fernando earthquake, residents went days or weeks without water, gas, heat and telephone service, and many houses were condemned. The 1994 Northridge earthquake caused fires that burned more than 70 homes in the neighborhood.

The practical lesson is to check water lines after strong shaking. Look at the water heater connections, the angle stops under sinks and the washing machine hoses, then watch the meter for movement with everything off. Townsite development started with a 1922 subdivision and accelerated after the 1963 freeway interchange, so a lot of housing here is decades old, and decades-old supply lines deserve attention.
