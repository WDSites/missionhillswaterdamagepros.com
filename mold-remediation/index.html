Mold needs moisture to grow, so the leak or humidity source has to be fixed before remediation can succeed. The work follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Health questions about mold belong to your physician.

Musty smell, dark spots or a ceiling stain: is it mold?

People usually do not search for remediation first. They search for a musty smell in a closet, black spots in a bathroom corner, a stain that keeps coming back or fuzzy growth behind furniture. All of those can be mold, and all of them point to moisture that was never fully dried.

Mold is not always visible, and not every dark spot is mold. An inspection with a moisture meter and a camera can show where moisture is and whether growth is likely behind a surface. That inspection is where this page begins, and the rest covers what comes next.

Bathrooms

Mold shows in corners of shower walls and ceilings, often from a missing fan or a slow leak.

Closets and exterior walls

Cool walls and little air movement hold moisture, especially behind furniture.

Under sinks

A slow drip can keep cabinet bases damp for months.

Ceilings below a leak

Roof, AC condensate line or upstairs plumbing leaks wet insulation and drywall.

Mold after water damage

The most common route to a mold job is an unresolved water loss. A toilet leak above a finished room, a roof or siding leak, a clogged AC condensate line or a slow sink drip can all wet materials long enough for mold to grow. When the leak is fixed and the area is dried promptly, mold often never appears.

If you had water damage recently, read about water mitigation and water damage cleanup, because proper drying is the best prevention. If drying was late or incomplete, an inspection can tell you whether mold is present. No honest company can promise that mold will never return, because that depends on moisture control afterward.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

Remediation starts with containment. Plastic barriers and negative air machines with HEPA filtration keep spores from drifting into the rest of the home during work. Technicians in protective gear remove moldy porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet, and clean or sand hard surfaces.

Black mold is a common name for dark-colored growth, and color alone does not tell you how serious it is. The same containment and removal process applies. EPA guidance says small areas of less than about 10 square feet can often be cleaned by a homeowner, while larger areas, extensive water damage and sewage-related growth call for professionals.

Porous materials such as ceiling tile and carpet that become moldy may need to be thrown away, because growth extends into the material. Hard surfaces can be scrubbed with detergent and water and dried completely. Painting or caulking over mold is not a fix, because the paint is likely to peel.

Mold inspection, testing and clearance

Inspection looks for moisture sources and visible growth. Testing, where it is used, samples air or surfaces to show what is present. Many callers ask for independent clearance testing, which is a check done after remediation to confirm the area is clean enough.

Independent clearance means the person who tests is not the same company that removed the mold, which keeps the check unbiased. Clearance matters in practice. For example, a home sale or loan may require proof that mold was removed. We cannot promise what a lender or buyer will accept, so ask them what documentation they need.

Mold remediation questions

Can I remove mold myself?

Small areas, under about 10 square feet according to EPA guidance, can often be cleaned by a homeowner. Larger areas, widespread water damage and anything involving sewage should be handled by professionals.

Will mold come back after remediation?

It can if moisture is not controlled. Remediation removes growth, but only fixing the water source and keeping the area dry keeps it from returning.

Do I need mold testing?

Not always. A moisture inspection and visual assessment may be enough, and testing is used when growth is hidden or documentation is needed. Ask what the testing would add.

Does a home sale or loan require mold clearance?

Sometimes. A lender or buyer may ask for proof that mold was removed. Independent clearance testing provides documentation, but we cannot promise what a lender will accept.

Is mold dangerous to my family?

Health questions belong to your physician. If you have health concerns, talk to a doctor before work starts and keep children and sensitive people away from the work area.
