When storm season brings water to Pacoima

Most Pacoima water losses begin the way they do elsewhere, with a pipe, a hose or a roof. In winter storms the roof and drainage lines are tested hardest, and a clogged gutter or a blocked yard drain can send water toward the house. Check your roof, gutters and yard drains before the rains, and call quickly if water gets in.

Water that enters from outside is a different category of job. Runoff carries soil and contaminants, so it is handled under the flood damage restoration page with a safety plan. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood damage, so check your coverage before the season starts.

If water is still spreading in Pacoima

Stop what you can

Close the main water valve. If water is entering from outside, do not wade out to the street to find the cause.

Mind the power

Turn off breakers in wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. Otherwise stay out and call 911 or the utility.

Sandbag awareness

If you use sandbags, place them on the outside of door thresholds. Do not step into moving water to reach them.

Record it

Photograph the water level, the entry point and anything damaged before you start cleaning.

The wash, the dam and the reservoir

The Pacoima Wash runs through the neighborhood and was part of the original land subdivision, and Pacoima Dam sits upstream. Los Angeles County Public Works manages the dam and reservoir. The county says sediment has filled about 60% of the original reservoir capacity, and it is reviewing a sediment-removal project, with a second phase tentatively scheduled to start in late 2027.

That is a county flood-control matter, not a prediction about your street. The useful step for homeowners is to check the FEMA flood map for their address and ask their insurer about flood coverage. Ask the City of Los Angeles for anything specific about your block.

Housing built in the 1950s and 1960s

Pacoima grew into a bedroom community in the 1950s, helped by the Golden State Freeway, and the greatest single-family construction came in the early 1950s to early 1960s. Los Angeles annexed the neighborhood in 1915, and a 1955 city project added curbs, sidewalks and streets. Many homes here are therefore 60 or more years old.

Homes of that age may still have original supply lines of older materials, aging water heaters and roofs that have been patched. If your home has never had its supply lines checked, the cost of a plumber visit is small compared with a soaked floor. Mention the age of the home when you call, because it affects the plan.
