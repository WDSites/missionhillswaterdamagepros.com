A crawl space is the low, unfinished area under a raised floor. Water that gets in there is easy to miss, because nobody goes down to look. In Mission Hills, CA crawl spaces are less common than slabs, and basements are rare, so this page is for the older raised-floor homes that have them. Call (818) 337-2642 if you see standing water or smell dampness under the house.

We pump out water and dry the space, the joists and the subfloor above it. Grading, drainage repairs and plumbing fixes are separate trades. If your house sits on a slab, slab leak water damage is the more likely page for you.

How water gets under a raised-floor house

Southern California gets little rain in most years, but heavy storms can drop a lot in a day, and runoff finds low spots around foundations. A dry crawl space in September can hold standing water after a single winter storm.

Common causes include plumbing leaks that drip for months, clogged or missing drainage, and soil sloped toward the house. A leaking supply or drain line over a crawl space rarely shows up inside the home until floors feel soft.

Ventilation and access rules that matter

The California Residential Code sets crawl space ventilation by area, generally one square foot of vent opening for every 150 square feet of floor, with provisions for reducing that when a vapor retarder covers the ground. It also calls for an access opening of at least 18 by 24 inches through the floor. Check the current code with the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety, since local amendments and your home age can change what applies.

Blocked vents trap humid air and keep the space damp. A trapdoor that has been painted shut or covered by furniture also delays the day you notice a problem.

Signs of crawl space water trouble

Musty smell in the house

Odors rising through floor registers or baseboards often come from damp soil or wet framing.

Soft or bouncy floor

Wet subfloor loses stiffness, and boards may sag between joists.

Standing water or dark soil

Puddles, mud and a wet vapor barrier show water is collecting below.

Pests and white fuzz

Insects, rot and fuzzy growth on joists point to long-term moisture.

First hour crawl space safety steps

  1. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking, and turn off power to any equipment in the space.
  2. Do not enter standing water near wiring. Call and describe what you see from the access opening.
  3. Wear a mask and gloves if you look, because crawl spaces can hold pests, droppings and mold.
  4. Photograph the water, the vents and any damp framing from a safe position.
  5. Call the 24/7 line so pumping and drying can begin.

Tight spaces are hazardous

Crawl spaces have low clearance and uneven footing. Do not go in alone, and never go in if you smell gas or see exposed wiring in water.

How we dry a crawl space

1

Pump out the low spots

Submersible pumps and wet vacuums take standing water from the soil depressions between piers.

2

Read the wood

A moisture meter checks joists, beams and the plywood underside of the floor.

3

Move air through

Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed so air passes along the joist bays.

4

Check again

Wood and soil readings are repeated each visit until they settle into a normal range.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard that describes how wet framing is dried, and it is named here as a reference alone. If water sat long enough to grow mold, see mold remediation.

What changes the cost of crawl space work

Access and clearance

A low, tight space makes every task slower.

Amount of water

Deep standing water takes more pumping than damp soil.

Insulation and vapor barrier

Wet insulation and torn barriers may need removal.

Duration

Long-term moisture raises the chance of rot and mold.

Insurance fit

Sudden plumbing failures under a home are often covered, while groundwater, seepage and long-term moisture often are not. Flood and surface water are generally a separate policy. Photograph the space and note the date you found the water before anything is moved.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not ignore a musty smell coming from the floor.
  • Do not block vents with storage or soil.
  • Keep gutters and downspouts pointing water away from the foundation.
  • Have a plumber check supply and drain lines under the house on a schedule.

Water that came from a heavy storm is covered at storm water damage.
