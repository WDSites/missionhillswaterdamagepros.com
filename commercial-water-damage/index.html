How water losses happen in commercial buildings

The usual sources are supply line failures, roof or window leaks, sprinkler or fixture damage and sewer backups. Commercial spaces often have long runs of concealed plumbing above ceilings and inside walls, so a leak can travel a long way before it is seen. Tenants may notice stained ceiling tiles, wet carpet or a smell before anyone traces the source.

OSHA's walking-working surfaces rule requires that workroom floors be kept clean and, to the extent feasible, dry. It also requires hazards to be corrected or blocked off before use. A wet floor in a place of work is therefore both a property problem and a safety one, and closing off the area promptly is the first practical step.

Signs of commercial water damage

Stained or sagging ceiling tiles

Discolored tiles point to a leak above the ceiling grid.

Wet carpet or lifting flooring

Moisture under floor coverings can spread under walls into other suites.

Musty odor in a closed space

Damp materials give off odors even when surfaces look dry.

Condensation or water in the lowest area

Water gathering at floor drains or low corners shows where the problem is draining.

First hour safety steps for a commercial loss

  1. Stop the source if you can, using the building main or the shutoff for that line.
  2. Block off wet floors and post a warning so no one slips, in line with OSHA requirements.
  3. Keep people away from electrical panels that stand near water, and cut power only where the panel is dry.
  4. Move inventory, equipment and records out of the wet area where it is safe to do so.
  5. Call building management or the owner, your insurer and the 24/7 line.

Sewage and electrical hazards come first

If wastewater is involved or electrical equipment is in standing water, keep everyone out and call the right party first.

Who is responsible for what

In a leased space, the lease usually sets who handles the building structure and who handles contents and improvements. A sewage problem may involve the private lateral, which LA Sanitation says the property owner maintains out to the middle of the street. Ask your landlord or property manager early so that responsibility and access are settled before work starts.

What changes the cost of commercial water damage work

Floor area

More square footage affected means more equipment and more monitoring.

Materials

Carpet tiles, suspended ceilings, drywall and concrete each dry differently.

Access and schedule

Work in occupied spaces may require phasing or after-hours visits.

Water category

Clean, gray and black water call for different cleanup steps.

Insurance fit and documentation

Commercial property policies and tenant policies split coverage in different ways, and business interruption is usually a separate matter. Photograph the damage, keep moisture logs and ask your insurer what documentation it needs before equipment leaves. Gradual damage is often treated differently from a sudden failure.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not leave wet carpet tiles or ceiling tiles in place hoping they dry.
  • Do not wait for the landlord to act if the water is still spreading.
  • Know where the water main and the local shutoffs are for your suite.
  • Walk the space after storms and check ceilings and floor drains.
