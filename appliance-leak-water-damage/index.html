Appliance leaks are the quiet kind of water loss. A washing machine hose seeps behind the unit, a dishwasher drips under the cabinet or a refrigerator line weeps for weeks, and the first sign is a soft floor or a musty smell. In Mission Hills, CA, call (818) 337-2642 when you find water near an appliance and tell us what is wet.

This page is about the water damage that appliances cause in laundry rooms, kitchens and utility areas. Fixing the appliance is a job for its owner or a repair technician. We extract water and dry the floor, walls and cabinets around it.

Where appliance leaks come from

ApplianceCommon failureWhere water goes
Washing machineSupply hose, drain hose or door sealLaundry floor, wall behind the unit, adjacent rooms
DishwasherDoor gasket, supply fitting or drain lineCabinet base, toe-kick and flooring
RefrigeratorIce maker supply line or drain panFloor beneath and behind, often unseen
Sink or disposal plumbingSlip joints and trapsCabinet floor and subfloor

Rubber hoses harden with age and burst, and a supply line left under pressure can fail while no one is home. LADWP guidance notes that older Los Angeles homes may have no pressure regulator at all, and that the owner is responsible for keeping one working. Higher pressure puts more strain on every hose and fitting that connects to an appliance.

Mineral scale can also matter. Hard water can build up inside valves and fittings, and LADWP published water quality data for the Valley puts total hardness near 103 mg/L.

Signs of a hidden appliance leak

Soft or warped flooring

Laminate edges lift and hardwood cups where water has wicked in beneath the appliance.

Musty smell in the laundry room

A smell with no visible puddle can mean water is trapped under or behind the unit.

Stains on the cabinet floor

Dark staining or swelling inside a sink or dishwasher cabinet shows slow seepage.

High water bill

A bill that climbs with no change in use can point to a seeping line.

First hour appliance leak safety steps

  1. Close the shutoff valve at the wall behind or under the appliance. If there is no valve, close the main.
  2. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and your footing are dry. If they are not, leave it plugged in and call.
  3. Do not move a washer or refrigerator through standing water.
  4. Mop up pooled water, lift rugs and photograph the source and the damage.
  5. Call the 24/7 line so drying can begin before water reaches the subfloor.

How we dry an appliance leak

1

Lift the water

The floor is extracted first, and the machine is moved only when it can be done safely.

2

Check below and behind

Readings under the flooring, in the wall behind the unit and inside the neighboring cabinet base show where seepage went.

3

Make small openings

A toe-kick or a length of baseboard is removed so air can reach the damp space.

4

Verify against a dry area

Drying continues until the readings match an unaffected part of the same room.

The trade standard on drying is ANSI/IICRC S500, listed here as reading material and not as a credential. Kitchen cabinets and countertops get their own treatment on the kitchen water damage page.

What changes the cost of an appliance leak

How long it leaked

Weeks of seepage mean more subfloor and more materials than a one-time spill.

Flooring type

Tile, vinyl, laminate and carpet each hold water differently.

Cabinet and wall involvement

Water that reached cabinetry or drywall adds scope.

Access

Tight spaces behind built-in units add labor.

Insurance fit

The California Department of Insurance found water losses were about 36 percent of homeowners claims in a study of 2000 to 2002 data from 13 large insurers. A sudden hose burst is often covered, but long-term seepage or a neglected fitting often is not. Keep the failed part if you can, photograph the area and ask your insurer what documentation they need.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not ignore a small puddle behind a machine.
  • Replace rubber supply hoses with braided ones on a schedule, and check them each year.
  • Do not leave a washer running while you are away.
  • Know where each appliance shutoff is and test it.

If the leak came from a pipe in the wall instead, see burst pipe water damage.

Appliance leak questions

Is emergency appliance leak water damage service available in Mission Hills, CA?

Yes. Close the valve behind the machine, then call at any hour. Same-day dispatch is offered when a crew is free.

Do you repair the appliance?

No. A repair technician or the owner deals with the machine. Our job is the wet floor, wall and cabinet around it.

Can a slow washer leak damage the subfloor?

Yes. Water can seep into the subfloor and the bottom of the wall for weeks, so a moisture inspection is worth doing even when the surface looks dry.

Will my floor need replacing?

It depends on the material and how long it stayed wet. Tile often survives while laminate and soft wood often do not.
