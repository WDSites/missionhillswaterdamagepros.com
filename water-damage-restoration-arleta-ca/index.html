Arleta is a neighborhood in the City of Los Angeles, in the northeast San Fernando Valley, near Mission Hills. If water is spreading in your Arleta home, call (818) 337-2642 and say what is wet. This page covers how local conditions affect a water loss and which service pages apply.

Where Arleta sits

The Arleta Neighborhood Council gives the official boundaries. The Pacoima Wash forms the north edge between Woodman Avenue and the I-5. Roscoe Boulevard and the Tujunga Wash form the south edge, the I-5 and Branford Street mark the east, and Woodman Avenue marks the west. The neighborhood was recognized as Arleta in 1968.

Arleta shares ZIP 91331 with Pacoima, so a ZIP code alone does not tell a dispatcher which neighborhood you live in. Give your street address and nearest cross street instead. Residents also name Branford Park and Devonshire Arleta Park as local landmarks.

Two flood channels and why they matter

Two concrete flood control channels touch Arleta. The Pacoima Wash runs along the north edge and joins the Tujunga Wash, which runs along the southern edge. Both are engineered channels built to carry storm runoff toward the Los Angeles River.

The channels carry stormwater. They do not mean your home floods. Rain damage here usually has ordinary causes, such as a roof leak, a clogged gutter, water pushed under a garage door or a failed drain. Fences along the channels exist because fast water in a concrete channel is dangerous, so keep children away from them during rain.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. To see how an individual address is mapped, enter it in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Roof and gutter leaks

Stains that grow after rain often start at the roof. See roof leak water damage.

Garage and door entry

Runoff that pushes under a door counts as water from outside, so say so on the call.

Plumbing failures

Dry-weather leaks usually come from supply lines, heaters or appliances inside the house.

Drain backups

Wastewater coming up a floor drain needs protective handling, and sewage backup cleanup explains it.

Housing age and what to check

Neighborhood-specific housing age data was not verified for this page, so it makes no claim about when Arleta homes were built. The safer approach is to check your own house. Find out when your plumbing was last replaced, look at the water heater label for its age, and inspect washing machine hoses and under-sink connections.

A slow leak can be present for weeks. Watch your water meter with every fixture off. If the dial still moves, a line is leaking somewhere, and that can point to a hidden leak, including one under a concrete slab.

First steps when water is spreading

Close the main valve

Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not move, call anyway and tell the dispatcher.

Protect yourself

Cut power to wet rooms only while standing on dry ground. If water has reached outlets or the panel, keep out and phone 911 or the utility.

Photograph the damage

Record the source, the wet areas and damaged belongings before anything is moved.

Lift what you can

Move small furniture, papers and electronics off wet floors.

Extraction, drying and documentation

The work starts with finding the source and removing standing water. Wet drywall, pad and flooring are tested with a moisture meter, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure over several days. Many contractors follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for this work, and you can ask any company how it documents readings.

For your insurer, keep photos, dates, receipts and a list of damaged items. Ask what documentation is needed and what your policy covers. Standard homeowners policies often treat flood differently from a sudden pipe failure, so ask before deciding how to describe the loss.

Arleta water damage questions

Does Arleta have its own ZIP code?

Arleta shares ZIP 91331 with Pacoima. Give your street address and cross street when you call, because the ZIP alone does not identify the neighborhood.

Do the nearby flood channels mean my home is at risk?

Not by themselves. Flood-zone status is address-specific and was not verified for this page, so check your exact address with the FEMA map service.

Do you serve Arleta from Mission Hills?

Yes, Arleta is a neighboring Los Angeles neighborhood. Call with your address and the dispatcher will confirm what is possible for your location.

Who handles water service and permits in Arleta?

Arleta is part of the city of Los Angeles, so LADWP is the water utility. For bigger repairs, the permit office is LADBS.

What if the water came from rain?

Say so on the call. Water from outside is handled and often insured differently from a pipe leak, so photograph where it entered and ask your insurer.
