A slab leak is a pipe failure under or inside the concrete floor of a house. The water has nowhere obvious to go, so it rises through cracks, soaks flooring from below and sometimes runs for weeks before anyone sees it. In Mission Hills, CA many homes sit on concrete slabs, so this is a real local loss. Call (818) 337-2642 if a floor feels warm or damp for no reason.

We extract the water and dry the floor, walls and cabinets it reached. Finding and repairing the pipe is a job for a licensed plumber, and often involves leak detection equipment we do not use. Both jobs are needed, and starting drying early protects the finish materials.

How slab leaks happen in San Fernando Valley houses

The United States Geological Survey records the Northridge earthquake of January 17, 1994 as a magnitude 6.7 event that heavily damaged communities across the San Fernando Valley. Ground movement stresses slabs and the pipes embedded in or beneath them, and a line that shifted then can wear through years later. We cannot say any one leak traces to that quake, only that slab plumbing here has lived through real shaking.

Pressure plays a part as well. The Los Angeles Department of Water and Power calculates pressure at an address from its ground elevation, so some streets run higher than others. A pipe that rubs against concrete or rock under high pressure can pinhole from the outside, even when the water inside is not especially corrosive.

Signs of a slab leak

Warm spot on the floor

A patch that feels warm under bare feet can mean a hot water line is leaking beneath it.

Sound of running water

A faint hiss or trickle with every fixture closed points to a pressurized line.

Damp carpet or lifting flooring

Moisture coming up from below shows as damp edges, cupping boards or buckled planks.

Rising water bill

A bill that climbs while use stays the same suggests water is leaving the system unseen.

You can test for a leak by shutting off every fixture and watching the water meter. If the dial moves, a line is losing water somewhere between the meter and the house. A plumber can narrow it to the slab with pressure tests and listening equipment.

First hour safety steps for a slab leak

  1. Shut the water supply at the main, then watch the meter to see whether it stops.
  2. Treat outlets in damp rooms as live. Stay out of standing water near them, and cut a circuit only if the panel itself is dry.
  3. If a hot water line is the source, turn off the water heater to avoid damage and scalding.
  4. Move rugs, furniture and electronics off the damp floor and photograph what you see.
  5. Call a plumber for detection and the 24/7 line for extraction and drying.

Warm floors and electricity do not mix

Water under a slab can reach electrical conduits and outlets. If you feel a tingle near wet areas or smell something burning, leave and call 911.

How we dry a house after a slab leak

1

Remove the surface water

Extraction equipment takes water off the floor and out of the carpet near the rising spot.

2

Follow it sideways

Readings along baseboards and the bottom of nearby walls show how far water wicked from the slab.

3

Lift what traps moisture

Flooring that seals water against concrete is lifted, and baseboards come off at the wall bottoms.

4

Dry the slab and walls

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until slab surface readings and wall readings reach the house reference.

Concrete dries slowly and can hold moisture for a long time, so patience matters before new flooring goes in. Moisture targets for concrete and finish materials are discussed in ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard we list as a reference and not as a certification. Rebuilding afterward is on the water damage repair page.

What changes the cost of slab leak cleanup

Flooring type

Tile, vinyl, laminate, hardwood and carpet react differently to water from below.

Duration

A leak that ran for weeks soaks walls and cabinets, and may support mold.

Area affected

A single room is far simpler than a leak that spread through a hallway and bedrooms.

Access to the wall bottoms

Cabinets and built-ins add labor when water has reached them.

Insurance fit for a slab leak

Policies vary a great deal on slab leaks. Some cover the resulting water damage but not the cost of reaching and repairing the pipe, and many exclude damage from continuous seepage over time. Ask your insurer about the exact wording, keep the plumber report, and photograph the damage before drying equipment arrives.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not ignore a warm patch of floor or a steady rise in the water bill.
  • Do not cover damp flooring with a rug and hope it dries.
  • Ask your plumber about pipe age and pressure, since both raise slab leak risk.
  • Keep the plumber report with your home records.

Slab leak questions

Is emergency slab leak water damage service available in Mission Hills, CA?

Yes. Call whenever you notice the problem, even late at night. Close the main valve first, and a crew is dispatched the same day if one is free.

Do you fix the leaking pipe in the slab?

No, locating and repairing the line is plumbing work. We take over once it is stopped and dry the floor and walls.

How do I know the leak is under the slab?

Warm floor spots, a moving meter with fixtures closed and damp flooring with no other source are common signs. A plumber confirms it with testing.

Can the floor be dried without removing it?

Sometimes. Tile and sealed surfaces can often be dried in place, while glued hardwood and laminate often have to come up.
