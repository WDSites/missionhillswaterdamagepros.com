Mid-century homes and what their plumbing does

Granada Hills is made up mostly of mid-century homes, including the Balboa Highlands tract built by Joseph Eichler and the Knollwood Grove tract. Homes of that age have decades of use on their supply lines, drain lines, water heaters and roofs. A part that has worked for 50 years is not automatically about to fail, but it deserves inspection after any wet event.

Mid-century designs also bring construction details that matter for water. Some have flat or low-slope roofs that pool water, and some have heating pipes embedded in the slab. If your home has in-slab heating, a leak there is a specialist repair, and the restoration crew will dry around it and coordinate with the right trade.

Before we arrive in Granada Hills

Water off first

Close the main valve. If the leak is on a landscape line, close the valve at the irrigation controller.

Dry-ground rule for power

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only from dry ground. Wet floors near the panel mean stay out and call 911 or the utility.

Move and document

Lift rugs and electronics and take photos or video of everything wet.

Protect surfaces

Place foil or plastic under furniture legs on wet hardwood to avoid stains.

Streets and landmarks around Granada Hills

Residents describe their homes by Balboa Boulevard, Devonshire Street, Zelzah Avenue and Chatsworth Street. White Oak Avenue is a city historic-cultural monument for its 101 Deodar cedars, O'Melveny Park covers 672 acres in the hills, and Granada Hills Charter High School sits on Zelzah. The Ronald Reagan Freeway (SR-118) runs through the southern part of the neighborhood.

Use those references when you call. Say which side of Balboa or Devonshire you are on and the nearest cross street. If your home sits in the hills near O'Melveny Park, mention whether the road is steep or gated.

Water infrastructure close to home

Granada Hills is home to Van Norman Reservoir, the southern end of the Los Angeles Aqueduct, which carries water from the Owens Valley. That infrastructure belongs to the city utility and does not change your private plumbing, but it reflects how central water is to this area. Water service in the neighborhood comes from the Los Angeles Department of Water and Power.

For a rebuild after a loss, permits go through the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety, and rules vary by the kind of work. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage if you live near a drainage channel. See flood damage restoration if water entered from outside.
