Kitchens combine water, appliances, cabinets and flooring in a small space, so a leak there reaches expensive materials quickly. A dishwasher line, a sink trap or a refrigerator supply can soak a cabinet base before anyone notices. In Mission Hills, CA call (818) 337-2642 if a kitchen floor is wet or cabinets are swelling.

Why kitchens are hit hard

Water inside a kitchen travels under cabinet kick plates and into the subfloor, where it cannot evaporate. Particleboard cabinet boxes swell and crumble when wet, while solid wood and plywood hold up better. The American Community Survey reports a median household income of $94,215 in ZIP 91345, and many owners invest in kitchen remodels, which raises the value at risk when water gets in.

What you may notice

Swelling cabinet base

Particleboard bulges and the laminate peels where water has soaked in from below.

Buckled flooring

Laminate, vinyl planks and wood lift near the sink and dishwasher.

Dark staining under the sink

A black or brown ring in the cabinet floor means water has been present a while.

Sour or musty smell

Odor with no puddle can mean water is trapped under the floor or behind the cabinets.

First hour kitchen safety steps

  1. Close the shutoff under the sink or at the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main.
  2. Cut the kitchen circuits at the panel only if the area around it is dry.
  3. Unplug small appliances on wet counters only if your hands and footing are dry.
  4. Empty the cabinets under the sink and pull out wet items so air can reach the cabinet floor.
  5. Photograph everything, then call the 24/7 line.

Gas ranges and wet floors

A gas smell means you leave the house and phone 911 from outside. Keep switches and flames away from a gas range sitting in water.

How we dry a wet kitchen

1

Clear the floor and cabinet bases

Extraction takes water from the floor and from the bottom of the cabinets.

2

Probe the boxes and subfloor

Readings inside cabinet boxes, in the wall behind them and in the subfloor show what is wet.

3

Open carefully

Toe-kicks come off and small holes are drilled so air can enter, which keeps intact cabinetry where it can be saved.

4

Dry and record

Drying equipment runs and readings are logged until the cabinets and floor are dry.

Cabinet drying decisions are described in general terms by ANSI/IICRC S500, which we list as a reference only. Countertops are rarely harmed by water, but the cabinet boxes under them can be. Rebuilding is covered on the water damage repair page.

What changes the cost of kitchen water damage work

Cabinet material

Particleboard often cannot be saved once wet, while plywood and solid wood often can.

Flooring

Tile on a slab differs from wood or laminate over a subfloor.

Spread

Water that reached the dining room or hallway adds equipment.

Time wet

A day-old spill costs less to fix than weeks of seepage.

Insurance fit

A sudden pipe or appliance failure is often covered, while slow leaks under a sink often are not. Insurers usually want photos of the source and the damage before cabinets are removed. Keep the failed part and receipts for any emergency work.

Mistakes to avoid and prevention

  • Do not leave wet items in the cabinet under the sink.
  • Do not seal the floor with caulk or new flooring until it has been measured dry.
  • Check under the sink and behind the dishwasher every few months.
  • Replace old supply lines and braided hoses on a schedule.

Is emergency kitchen water damage restoration available in Mission Hills, CA?

Yes. Close the shutoff under the sink or at the appliance, then call at any hour. A crew is sent the same day if one is free.

Can wet cabinets be saved?

Sometimes. Plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Particleboard that has swollen usually has to be replaced.

Will my countertop be damaged?

Stone and solid surfaces usually are not affected by water. The cabinets and walls under them are what get wet.

Do you do kitchen remodels?

No, remodeling and cabinet installation belong to other trades. We stop at a dry, measured kitchen.
