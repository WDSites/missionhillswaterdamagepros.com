Apartments, rentals and shared walls

Panorama City mixes single-family homes with low-rise apartments, and as of the 2010 census, 64.8% of units were renter-occupied. That changes how a water loss unfolds. The leak may start in a unit you do not control, and the water may travel down walls and ceilings into yours.

If you rent, tell your landlord or manager in writing and photograph the damage. If you own, check the association rules before work starts. Tell the dispatcher whether the building has a shutoff for each unit or one building valve, because that decides who can stop the water.

A planned community with its own history

Developer Fritz Burns and industrialist Henry J. Kaiser launched Panorama City in 1948, after farmland gave way to postwar housing. Many homes were financed through FHA and G.I. Bill loans, and the community became a model for Valley development. The result is a grid of similar homes and buildings from the same era.

A housing stock built around the same time means similar plumbing ages, and a leak in one building often repeats in the next. If your home or complex was built in the late 1940s or 1950s, ask when the supply lines and water heater were last replaced. Older shut-off valves can seize, so test them once a year.

Streets and places in Panorama City

Residents talk about Van Nuys Boulevard, Roscoe Boulevard, Nordhoff Street and Woodman Avenue. The Kaiser Permanente medical complex at Woodman and Roscoe, Panorama Mall and the Sepulveda Pool are well-known reference points. A light rail station is planned on Nordhoff Street.

Woodman Avenue also runs through Mission Hills, where a 72-inch trunkline break flooded the street in 2019. Tell the dispatcher the nearest cross street so the crew reaches the right block.

Large buildings bring their own risks. Panorama Mall and the former Ohrbach's building, now the Valley Indoor Swap Meet, are the kind of wide, low structures where a sprinkler line or roof drain failure can wet a lot of floor area. If you manage a shop or a warehouse space, say so on the call, give the square footage and tell dispatch whether the space must reopen quickly.

Commercial losses also involve tenants, landlords and business insurance. Keep a written timeline, photograph inventory before it is moved and ask your insurer what proof of loss it needs. The crew can document moisture readings by area to help with that paperwork.
